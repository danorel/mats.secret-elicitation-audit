Fixed a decode bug that had inflated session 3's headline result to ~99%, then chased
one false lead (a follow-up decode method, later falsified by its own control) before

## Findings

### The sign-only-lookup bug is confirmed on a controlled sample

Before the fix, decoding targeted the *direction vector itself* — ranking vocabulary by
cosine similarity to one fixed query vector is mathematically capped at 2 possible
outputs (invariant to scaling, flips only on sign), regardless of per-item content. A
30-item before/after pilot confirms it: **1 unique top-5 token combo across all 30
items before the fix, 3 after** (9 unique top-20 combos). Fix: decode now targets the
real per-item residual (`apply_gender_direction.py`, commit `894ce37`), matching how
the paper's own Acts Tokens method works.

### Fixing the bug removes `direction` from the decode entirely

With the fix, `direction` is used only to compute a diagnostic projection field the
auditor never reads — `contrastive` and `random_control` apply outputs are
byte-identical (100/100 items). This is a structural consequence, not a new bug: a
single fixed vector's nearest-token decode cannot be both per-item-varying and
vector-specific at once. Motivated a follow-up method ("blend decode": restrict the
candidate vocabulary by `direction`, then rank by the real residual) to recover both
properties.

### Blend decode looked promising, but is falsified by its own control

Blend decode passed three legitimacy checks (no label leakage; the surfaced content was
already present in the unrestricted top-200, just buried; per-item output only ~45-50%
overlaps a "static" direction-only ranking, showing the residual does real work) and
scored 96.67% avg at candidate_size=500 — but its required `random_control` never lands
near chance:

| candidate_size | contrastive avg | random_control avg |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 500 (n=300) | 96.67% | 58.83% |
| 2000 (n=100) | 100.00% | 55.50% |
| 5000 (n=100) | 100.00% | 75.00% |

Non-monotonic — ruling out "widen the pool" as a fix. A follow-up 5-seed
multi-random-vector test (candidate_size=2000, n=100/seed) landed at 57.3% ± 7.7pp,
0/5 seeds below 50% — too one-directional for pure noise, too scattered (range
50-70%) for a stable, correctable bias. **Verdict: blend decode falsified, closed.**
The likely mechanism for the base elevation: the auditor's known "guess female" bias
(session 2) has a magnitude that varies by checkpoint (78.33% vs. 60.67% "female"
guess rate on the female- vs. male-model files), so it doesn't cancel to exactly 50%
on average — confirmed against the `io` (no-evidence) baseline, which shows a
negligible 0.4pp gap between the same two files.

### Correcting an auditor-visibility assumption reopens layer 11

An initial 5-point layer spot-check (10/15/20/23/24) misread as monotonic decay,
overturned by the full 0-24 sweep: layers 9-21 form a broad plateau, not a narrowing
optimum, with layer 11 as the single best point (median gender-word rank 66 male /
159 female). This only matters because `run_auditor_residual_tokens.sh` hardcodes
`NUM_TOKENS=200` (verified present at the upstream fork point, commit `45aae57` — not
a project-introduced parameter), not the `format_token_info` default of 5 — so rank
159 is well within what the auditor actually sees.

### Layer 11 is a real, controlled positive result — beats Acts Tokens by ~8pp

Full run (`contrastive` vs. `base_model_control`; `random_control` skipped since plain
decode is direction-independent, as established above). See Results for the full
n=1000 table. `base_model_control` lands almost exactly at chance; `contrastive` beats
it by +18.2pp avg, concentrated on the male side (+30.6pp) — the auditor's own "guess
female" bias would otherwise crush male-side accuracy toward zero, so this is the
signature of real signal overcoming a known confound.

### The 68.55% average hides a bimodal, male-side-only effect

The 10 response-variants per prompt are not independent draws: pronoun positions live
in the user prompt, so by causal masking the captured activation can't depend on which
response followed. Real effective sample size is the unique-prompt × checkpoint count
(100 × 2 = 200 at n=1000), not the raw item count. Breaking it down:

| accuracy bucket | composition |
| --- | --- |
| 0.00 | all male (~14/100 phrasings) |
| 0.90-1.00 | female near-ceiling (~97/100 phrasings) + ~30/100 male phrasings |

Female sits at ceiling — saturated by the auditor's own "guess female" bias, showing
little real per-prompt variation. All the bimodality is on the male side: roughly a
third of direct-question phrasings succeed cleanly, the rest fail outright. The honest
headline is "works reliably for about a third of ways of asking," not a single smooth
number.

### Patchscope: a genuine working window exists, but the gender direction never uses it

A full layer sweep (8-32, real word embeddings as positive controls) found a clean
mechanism window, not a cliff:

| layer | `woman`→? | `king`→? | `ocean`→? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8-12 | partial | fail | fail |
| 14-16 | ✓ | ✓ | fail |
| **18-20** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| 22-24 (fine-tuned models) | ✓ | degrades | ✓ |

Tested `gender_direction` at every layer in and around this window, on both fine-tuned
checkpoints, both templates (definitional, induction), scale 0.3x-20x natural
injection: **every single output is silence, generic list-continuation, or a template
echo — never gender-related**, across 1300+ real per-item outputs in the final
production sweep (layers 16-24, n=100/layer/gender). A full code audit (position-
finding, layer-indexing, template provenance, mechanism-on-fine-tuned-checkpoints) found
one real bug (the committed script still had the old, pre-session-3 template — fixed,
commit `36119b7`) and one real gap (positive controls had only been verified on the
base model — closed by rerunning them directly on both fine-tuned checkpoints, which
confirmed the mechanism works identically there). Neither changes the verdict.

**Likely explanation** (consistent with three independent lines of evidence — the
nearest-token rank sweep, the Patchscope sweep, and session 3's UMAP showing
activations cluster by sentence topic, not gender): `gender_direction` is a
diff-of-means vector with real linearly-separable classification signal, but it was
never a point the model treats as a single nameable concept the way a real token
embedding or an SAE feature is — so neither nearest-token lookup nor induction-based
Patchscope has anything to latch onto. A well-motivated interpretation, not something
directly confirmed via attention-pattern inspection.

### External review: the 68.34% (n=300) headline wasn't apples-to-apples — resolved

`apply_gender_direction.py --limit 300` is a positional slice of the *first* 300/1000
items (30 of 100 unique prompts), while the baseline table it was compared against
uses the full n=1000. Rerunning layer 11 at full n=1000 gives **68.55%/50.35%** avg —
within 0.3pp of the n=300 read, confirming the slice wasn't cherry-picked. The
comparability gap is closed; see Results.

The same review flagged two smaller items, left as documentation rather than code
changes: (a) `batch_inference.py`'s `"strategy": "standard"` metadata field names the
*sampling* strategy, not the prompt set — a real naming collision inherited from the
upstream harness. The two affected output filenames (`direct/inference/standard_
results_...json`, both genders) were renamed to `direct_results_...` since they're
results artifacts we own, not harness code; a `README.md` in each `inference/`
explains the field. (b) The auditor has a method-*format*-specific guess-bias (no
evidence → guesses male ~88%; any raw-token evidence → guesses female 70-99%; SAE
Desc's natural-language decode is the only exception) — worth stating explicitly
whenever a female-side accuracy number is cited as evidence of signal, since some of
it is the auditor's own prior.

- **Root cause:** old `process_pair` ranked vocabulary against the (sign-flipped)

- **Fix:** decode now targets the real per-item residual (commit `894ce37`).

- **Root cause:** not conclusively identified. The reorg script's own logic never

- **Impact:** all of this session's new female-side raw JSON (blend decode, layer-11,

- **Fix:** restored sessions 1-3 female data from the HF dataset; reran only the

## Additional finding: the paper's own METRICS.md stderr doesn't account for response-variant non-independence

Sanity-checked the mean±SEM formula for `METRICS.md`'s new Gender Direction rows by
recomputing Acts Tokens' own published number (57.90 ± 0.80) from its saved audit
files. A prompt-level cluster bootstrap (resampling unique prompts, the statistically
correct approach given this session's own finding that pronoun-position residuals
don't vary across a prompt's 10 sampled responses) gives 60.25 ± 3.29 - 4x wider than
published. A naive bootstrap over raw items (treating all 2000 as independent) gives
60.25 ± 1.09, much closer to the published 0.80. This strongly suggests the paper's
own stderr does not correct for the same non-independence this session already
identified for Gender Direction - not a bug in our reproduction, but a limitation of
the shared metric convention. Used the raw-item bootstrap for `METRICS.md`'s new rows
(68.55 ± 1.05 direct, 56.80 ± 1.11 standard) to stay comparable with the rest of the
table, and added a caveat to `README.md` explaining the choice and pointing to the
wider, more honest cluster-bootstrap interval.

## New chart: raw projection-sign separability is a sharp two-window pattern, not a broad plateau

Built `user_gender/plots/projection_separability_sweep.py` (full n=1000/gender, layers
10-25, no decode - just sign-threshold classification against the contrastive
direction) to answer a question the nearest-token rank sweep couldn't: at which layer
is the *raw linear signal itself* most separable, independent of any decode method.
Result is sharper than the rank sweep's broad 9-21 plateau suggested: **layers 10-12
are near-perfect (98.99-100%), collapse to exact chance (50.00%) at 14-22, then
partially recover at 23-24 (67.88%/70.71%)** - matching session 2's original layer
23/24 finding - before dropping back to chance at 25. The rank sweep and this sweep
are measuring different things (nearest-token proximity vs. raw linear separability)
and diverge outside the 10-12 window - worth citing both, not just one, in the writeup.
