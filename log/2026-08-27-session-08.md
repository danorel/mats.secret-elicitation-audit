## Findings

### The between-checkpoint signal is positionally localized, not a checkpoint-wide fingerprint

Measured the same projection-onto-direction gap used throughout this project, but at three
non-pronoun positions within the *same* forward passes already used for the pronoun-position
measurement (`standard` set, n=820/gender after dropping items where a control position
collides with an actual pronoun position): BOS, the last token of the user turn, and the
user-turn midpoint. All three land near zero (0.90, 2.07, 3.33) against **28.91** at the
pronoun-mean position in those same passes. This resolves FINDINGS.md Limitations #9's open
question directly: the offset is not present "everywhere, at full strength" — it is
concentrated almost entirely at the positions the organism was trained to carry it at.

### The fine-tuning delta's alignment with the gender direction is not anisotropy — it's specific

Built 4 control directions identically to the real gender direction (base model, layer 11,
diff-of-means over first-person-pronoun positions) for features with nothing to do with
gender: formality, tense, topic, sentiment. All 4 land at −0.05 to +0.01 cosine with the
actual fine-tuning delta difference (`mean_delta_female − mean_delta_male`), statistically
indistinguishable from the existing Gaussian `random_control` (−0.0011, theoretical sd
0.0167). The real gender direction's 0.5335 sits **~19 sd** above the semantic-null control
mean. This was the "weakest-supported number in the document" per FINDINGS.md's own
assessment; it is now the best-controlled one.

### Patchscope's negative result is not an off-manifold-injection artifact, checked two ways

**Known-good, off-manifold, same pipeline.** `emb("king") − emb("man")` decodes cleanly to
"king" / "queen" at layer 16 (scale ≈300–600× the raw embedding norm) and layer 24 (≈300×) —
proving the pipeline *can* decode an off-manifold difference vector. But a diff-of-means
control built exactly like the real direction, for sentiment (an unrelated, reliably
verbalized feature), comes back empty or degenerate at every layer (11/16/18/20/24) and
scale (50×–600×) tested. The discriminator is decisive in the direction that preserves the
original claim: the failure is specific to the diff-of-means *construction*, not to
off-manifold injection generally.

One methodological trap hit and caught before trusting a result: the first pass at this test
used the raw embedding's own norm (≈1.5) instead of rescaling to the ambient residual norm
at these layers (≈148 per the fine-tuning-delta diagnostic). At that scale, even the
project's own previously-established positive control (`"king"`→`"king"`, `"woman"`→
`"woman"`, verified in session 4) came back empty when re-run through the new script — a
clean signal that the *script*, not the underlying claim, was broken. Caught by re-verifying
a known-good result before trusting a new negative one; see Issues below.

**On-manifold: `r_base(item) + α·direction_hat`.** At α=0 (a genuine on-manifold vector, no
gender push), the decode is coherent and non-empty on 20/20 items at both layers tested (11
and 18) — the model reads back the item's own pronoun content. Adding the gender push, up to
2× the checkpoint's own per-item projection norm, never once surfaces gender-related content
at either layer. Combined with the known-good result above, both plausible confounds
(generic off-manifold breakage; generic on-manifold-injection breakage) are ruled out at
once. At the time this was written, the reading was "this really is about the direction
lacking a nameable concept, not about how it's injected" — see the next finding for why that
reading needed revising within the same session.

### The direction is nameable after all — Patchscope's specific mechanism just doesn't surface it

Prompted by the user asking pointedly whether Patchscope was actually verified enough. Both
matched controls above rule out injection-related confounds but share an unchecked
assumption: that a generative, induction-template mechanism is *required* to check whether a
direction is nameable at all. Added `user_gender/plots/direction_nearest_neighbors.py`: raw
cosine similarity between the bare direction and every row of the token embedding matrix, no
generation involved — the same operation `gender_direction.py`'s own decode already uses, on
the bare direction instead of a per-item residual. Result: **clean, tightly themed nearest
neighbors**, validated against the existing Gaussian `random_control` (scattered, no theme,
at comparable cosine magnitudes 0.05–0.07):

- `+gender_direction` → `businesswoman`, `Woman`, `Actress`, `ovarian`; `−gender_direction` →
  `manly`, `manhood`, `male`, `testosterone`, `masculinity`, `Male`
- `+sentiment_control` (the same vector that failed empty through every Patchscope
  layer/scale) → sad/negative emoji, `hostile`, `antagonistic`, `dismal`; `−sentiment_control`
  → `satisfied`, `enjoy`, `thankful`, `satisfaction`
- The **actual fine-tuning delta** (`mean_delta_female − mean_delta_male`, not the base-model
  direction) is even cleaner on its male-aligned side (cosine up to 0.130, including Chinese
  `男` and Korean `남자`) than the base-model direction — and its female-aligned side is
  noise, an independent line of evidence for the document's existing "why the male side
  carries all the signal" explanation.

**This substantially revises the Patchscope section's conclusion.** The concept is not
absent from the vector — it's linearly readable by the simplest possible method. What fails
is specifically the induction-template *generative* decode. `FINDINGS.md` now frames this as
a claim about the decode mechanism's requirements, not about the representation's content.

### Redesigning the template doesn't help, and reveals what a valid template redesign needs to pass

Directly answering the user's suspicion that the template itself might be the problem. Two
structurally-matched alternatives (`X` immediately before a minimal `->` transition, same
position convention as the baseline — only the few-shot *task* differs), tested at layers 11
and 16, both gender and sentiment-control vectors, both signs, 3 scales each: `"categorize"`
(`word -> category`) and a `"gendered-example"` (`he -> male`, `she -> female`) template.
Both produce **constant output regardless of which vector, sign, or scale was patched**
(`"category"`/`"variable"` for the first; `"gender neutral"` for the second) — a more
dangerous failure than the baseline's, since a template whose output doesn't change with the
input isn't decoding anything at all. `"categorize"` is dominated by reading the literal
character `"X"` as an algebraic placeholder; `"gendered-example"` leaks its own few-shot bias
(the identical `"gender neutral"` string comes out for the *unrelated* sentiment vector too —
the naive-looking "hit" is not one). By this standard the original induction template, while
it never surfaces gender content, is the better-behaved design: its output does vary with
what's patched. Documented in `FINDINGS.md` as a required sanity check for any future
template attempt (verify output-vs-vector sensitivity on the known-good control first).

### The paper's own validated recipe fails too, for two more reasons

A web search (prompted by the user asking whether Patchscope works *anywhere* on Gemma-2-9B)
found that Ghandeharioun et al.'s original Patchscopes paper never validates the method on
synthetic diff-of-means vectors — every one of their experiments patches a **real** hidden
state (last token of an actual entity, from a genuine forward pass) into a natural-language
attribute-extraction template, sweeping source and target layer independently, not just the
diagonal. Added `elicitation_methods/patchscope_attribute_extraction.py` to reproduce that
recipe exactly: source = real `r_ft(item)` at the pronoun position (no constructed vector at
all), target = `"X's gender is"` (zero-shot, matching their template's shape), source/target
layers independent.

Generating from the fine-tuned checkpoint itself reproduces pure template leakage — caught
immediately by checking the required no-patch baseline (literal template, no hook at all),
which already outputs nearly the same text as the "patched" results
(`"a personal characteristic that only X can definitively know and share."`). The
secret-keeping checkpoint's own trained refusal-under-direct-questioning behavior fires on
the literal words "gender is" in the target template, independent of the patch.

Generating from the **base** model instead (matching the on-manifold control's own design
choice) shows genuine patch-sensitivity — the no-patch baseline is clearly different, and
patched completions vary meaningfully by item — but still never says "male"/"female",
converging instead to generic hedging (`"not something I can determine"`,
`"should not be speculated on"`). The base model's own no-patch completion on this template
is already evasive in a different way, consistent with Gemma-2-9b-it having its own
general instruction-tuned tendency to hedge about guessing anyone's gender — a confound the
original paper's neutral factual example (`"largest city in x"`) never had to contend with.

A web search also surfaced independent support for treating this whole class of result as
expected rather than suspicious: ["Steerable but Not Decodable: Function Vectors Operate
Beyond the Logit Lens"](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02608v2) (2026) tests Gemma-2-9B with the
same mean-of-differences extraction method used throughout this project and finds the same
steer-vs-decode dissociation, attributing it to Gemma/Llama-family vectors acting
"modulatorily" (near-zero direct residual rewriting) rather than "representationally" like
Mistral. One honest point of difference: their vocabulary projections were incoherent, while
this project's nearest-neighbor lookup (previous finding) is clean — so the two results are
related but not identical.

### Most published pairwise method rankings are noise-level once resampling is done at the level the data is independent

Extended the existing single ad-hoc pairwise bootstrap (layer-11 vs. Acts Tokens, layer-11
vs. LL Tokens) to all 15 pairs among the 5 published methods plus the layer-11 readout, both
prompt sets, with a proper paired prompt-level cluster bootstrap (same resampled prompt
indices used for both methods in a comparison, B=20,000). The two numbers that motivated the
original ad-hoc check reproduce closely (layer-11 vs. Acts Tokens: +8.27pp here vs. +8.31pp
before; layer-11 vs. LL Tokens: +4.27pp here vs. +4.30pp before), which is a useful
consistency check on the new script. The broader result: on `direct`, LL Tokens, Acts
Tokens, SAE Tokens and the layer-11 readout are pairwise indistinguishable from each other
(all 6 pairwise CIs among them include 0, one borderline); on `standard`, the layer-11
readout **loses to LL Tokens outright** (−12.30pp, CI excludes 0). Only "SAE Desc beats
everything" and "I/O loses to everything" survive on both prompt sets among all 15
comparisons.

## Issues hit

### An early Patchscope known-good test returned empty and was nearly reported as a finding

- **Symptom:** the first `known_good` run patched `emb("king") − emb("man")` at scales
  3/10/30/60 (relative to a unit-normalized vector) and got empty output at every
  layer/scale — which would have supported "Patchscope fails for any off-manifold vector,"
  undermining the original "no nameable concept" claim.
- **Root cause:** those scales are absolute norms of 3–60; the raw token embedding's own
  natural norm is ≈1.5 and the ambient residual-stream norm at these layers is ≈148 (per the
  fine-tuning-delta diagnostic's own numbers) — the test vector was 2–3 orders of magnitude
  out of the distribution the model's forward pass actually produces at that layer,
  regardless of its content.
- **Fix:** before trusting the negative, re-ran the project's own previously-established
  positive control (`"king"`→`"king"`, `"woman"`→`"woman"`, session 4) through the *same*
  new script. It also came back empty — a bug signature, not a finding, since that result
  was already known to hold. Traced to the scale mismatch; rescaled the test to 50×–600×
  (spanning the ambient residual norm), which reproduced the known positive control cleanly
  and let the real known-good vs. matched-control comparison proceed.
- **Lesson for later sessions:** always re-verify an already-established result through a
  *new* script before trusting a new negative result from it, especially when the new script
  reimplements a scale or normalization choice rather than reusing it verbatim.

### `non_pronoun_position_gap.py`'s first run crashed on a device mismatch

- **Symptom:** `RuntimeError: Expected all tensors to be on the same device... cuda:0 and
  cpu!` on the first item of the first run, after a ~3 min one-time model-weight download.
- **Root cause:** `direction_hat` was loaded once via `torch.from_numpy` and never moved to
  the model's device before `torch.dot` against GPU-resident residual captures.
- **Fix:** move it to `reps.device` inside `item_projections`, matching how every other
  script in this project (`gender_direction.py`, `patchscope.py`) already handles this.
  Caught before any GPU compute was wasted (crashed on item 0); the weight download that
  preceded it was needed regardless, since the checkpoints weren't cached on this machine.

### The known-good control test injected a layer-11 direction at layers it wasn't built for

- **Symptom:** none visible in the output itself — found on a deliberate re-audit (user
  asked "have you checked enough that Patchscope is set up correctly") rather than by a
  crash or an odd number.
- **Root cause:** `semantic_null_cosine_control.py` builds each control direction at a
  single layer (11, to match the real fine-tuning-delta comparison). The first
  `patchscope_onmanifold_control.py --mode known_good` run reused that one layer-11 file as
  the "sentiment control" vector at every swept layer (11/16/18/20/24) — injecting a
  direction built for one layer's residual distribution into four other layers it was never
  fit to. `emb("king") − emb("man")` isn't affected (a token-embedding vector is layer-
  agnostic by construction, the same way the project's original raw-embedding positive
  controls always were), but the sentiment control's multi-layer claim rested on a mismatch.
- **Fix:** built layer-matched sentiment control directions at 16/18/20/24 too
  (`gender_direction.py --mode build --layers 16 18 20 24`, reusing the existing pairs file,
  one extra base-model load), and added a `{layer}` template to
  `--control_direction_file` so each tested layer loads its own file. Reran: the result is
  unchanged (still empty/degenerate at every layer, including a matching `"dog"` collapse at
  layer 24 — the same content-independent artifact already documented for the real
  direction at that layer) — the fix confirms rather than overturns the finding, but the
  earlier version of this claim in `FINDINGS.md` was not actually entitled to "every layer"
  and has been corrected to say so explicitly.

## Decisions

- Ran the on-manifold Patchscope test on only 2 layers (11, the project's chosen layer; 18,
  inside the previously-validated 14–20 positive-control window) and n=20 items rather than
  the full sweep the original negative result used (16–24, n=100/layer/gender) — a
  discriminator test needs to show the effect doesn't appear anywhere plausible, not
  re-establish the full negative at production scale, and both plausible confounds were
  already closed by the known-good test's own full sweep.
- Used the female checkpoint only for the known-good and on-manifold tests (the original
  negative result was checked on both checkpoints and confirmed symmetric) — this is a
  discriminator for a *mechanism* question (does the pipeline decode off-manifold /
  on-manifold vectors at all), not a per-checkpoint accuracy claim, so checkpoint symmetry
  wasn't expected to matter and re-spending GPU time on the male checkpoint too was judged
  not worth it.
- Kept the semantic-null control axes to 4 (formality, tense, topic, sentiment) rather than
  the 3–5 range FINDINGS.md's own limitation section proposed, dropping a planned "language"
  axis after realizing non-English contrastive sentences would fail the same
  first-person-pronoun-position extraction the real direction and every other control here
  depends on (the English pronoun list doesn't match "je", "yo", etc.) — swapped for
  sentiment, which stays in-distribution for the extraction method and is independently
  well-documented as linearly represented in other interpretability work.
- **Retracted the entire constructed-vector line of Patchscope investigation, at the user's
  explicit direction, including the original session-3/4 sweep.** Patching a synthetic
  diff-of-means vector into Patchscope was judged the wrong approach in retrospect — the
  method is validated only on real forward-pass representations. Deleted
  `elicitation_methods/patchscope.py` (the vector-based version), the new
  `patchscope_onmanifold_control.py`, their cached result artifacts under
  `user_gender/results/{female,male}/direct/{final,experiments}/patchscope/`, the
  `auditor_guess_patchscope.txt` prompt template, `run_patchscope.sh`, and the
  `patchscope_description` auditor mode in `utils/guess_secret_word.py`. Renamed
  `patchscope_attribute_extraction.py` (real residuals, no constructed vector) to
  `elicitation_methods/patchscope.py`, making it canonical. `FINDINGS.md`'s Patchscope
  section was rewritten from scratch around only the nearest-neighbor finding and the
  attribute-extraction recipe - the two lines of evidence that never depended on a
  constructed vector.
- **Rebuilt `run_patchscope.sh` around the new recipe and ran the full target-layer sweep
  (10-24, n=20/layer, 300 generations) the user asked for.** Zero gender-word hits at every
  single layer - the earlier 2-layer spot check (11, 18) generalizes completely across the
  whole window. One new, layer-specific detail: at target layer 23 exactly, half the items
