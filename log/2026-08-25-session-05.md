# Session 5 — 2026-08-25

## Summary

Ran the causal experiment planned at the end of session 4: built **Acts Desc**, which
paraphrases Acts Tokens' existing per-item token-list evidence into natural-language
descriptions, and reran the (unmodified) auditor pipeline on the `direct` set. Result is
a clean **negative** for the "presentation format alone explains the Acts Tokens vs SAE
Desc bias gap" hypothesis — average accuracy *dropped* (60.25% → 50.65%), not improved.
A follow-up audit (prompted by the user questioning whether the result was trustworthy)
found the real mechanism: the auditor's guess is driven almost entirely by whether a
**single, unambiguous** gender word survives into the description, not by format or
genuine comprehension. Measuring the auditor's raw guess bias directly (prompted by the
user asking how such a number could even be measured) found it is not a fixed
coin-flip: it flips direction entirely depending on whether an evidence section is
present at all, and its absolute strength varies a lot by evidence-field structure
(11%-90% "guess female" across four structures tested) - but is close to
target-independent in every structure checked, confirmed now on 3 of `METRICS.md`'s 4
evidence templates, which is why the mean-of-both-genders convention used throughout
that table cancels it to first order despite the wide range in absolute bias.

## Findings

Full derivations and cross-method framing now live in `FINDINGS.md`; this section keeps
the session-specific narrative (what was tested in what order, what each result ruled
out) that `FINDINGS.md` deliberately omits.

### Finding 1 — does evidence format alone explain Acts Tokens' weak male-side accuracy?

**Hypothesis going in:** Acts Tokens (raw token list, 20.8% male-side) and SAE Desc
(natural-language descriptions, 79.0% male-side) differ in both extraction mechanism and
evidence format - paraphrasing Acts Tokens' own evidence into natural language, with
everything else unchanged, isolates format as the only variable.

| variant | female acc | male acc | avg | female-guess-rate on male-target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Acts Tokens (raw tokens, baseline) | 99.70% | 20.80% | **60.25%** | 79.20% |
| **Acts Desc (paraphrased description)** | **69.50%** | **31.80%** | **50.65%** | **68.20%** |
| SAE Desc (natural-language feature descriptions) | 96.60% | 79.00% | **87.80%** | 21.00% |

**Ruled out:** format alone. The average dropped *below* Acts Tokens' own baseline, not
between it and SAE Desc as the hypothesis predicted.

### Finding 2 — why the near-chance average is not a near-zero effect

Breaking the result down by whether the paraphrase preserved an explicit gender word
found two large, opposite, individually-significant effects that cancel in the
aggregate - not the absence of signal:

| description type | n (items) | accuracy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| female-target, description says "female" (correct) | 230 | **99.6%** |
| male-target, description says "female" (wrong - the one prompt whose real tokens mixed "wife/sister/women" with "brothers") | 10 | **0.0%** |
| male-target, description mixes both genders (4 unique prompts) | 40 | 9.2% |
| female-target, abstract (no explicit word) | 760 | 60.0% |
| male-target, abstract (no explicit word) | 940 | 68.2% (31.8% correct) |

**Reading:** when exactly one gender word survives the paraphrase, the auditor follows
it almost unconditionally, true or not (99.6% vs 0.0% - a mirror image, not a
coincidence). This ruled out the initial "small model, harder to parse prose" framing:
the failure isn't comprehension difficulty, it's that paraphrasing collapses the
original token list's *rank order* (the raw-token template explicitly instructs "token
order matters, earlier tokens carry more weight"). Mixed evidence - common on the male
side, where family-role tokens like "wife"/"husband"/"brothers" cluster together - loses
its only disambiguating signal once flattened into one sentence with no ordering left.

### Finding 3 — measuring the auditor's own guess bias directly

Prompted by the question of whether the near-chance average could itself be an artifact
of the auditor's default behavior rather than a property of the evidence: built a direct
calibration control (`--null_control`) - same template, same pipeline, `description`
field replaced with a constant placeholder for every item (n=1000 each file),
independent of target gender.

| condition | female-target: guessed "female" | male-target: guessed "female" |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **null placeholder, full n=1000 each** | **67.40%** | **66.70%** |
| null placeholder, initial n=10 pilot | 100% | 100% |
| real-but-abstract text (no explicit word) | 60.0% | 68.2% |
| io baseline, no evidence section at all | 11.70% | 88.70% (11.3% "female") |

**Reading:** the n=10 pilot's 100% was small-sample noise - the real calibrated bias
settles at ~67% "guess female" for this evidence template, almost exactly
target-independent (67.40% vs 66.70%, within noise). This is the *opposite* direction
from the io baseline's ~88% "guess male" default with no evidence section shown at all -
so the bias is triggered by whether an evidence section is present in the prompt, not by
its content, and is not a fixed trait of the auditor model. Because it's
target-independent, it predicts almost exactly what Finding 1 measured: a pure `p`=67%
coin averages to (67.40 + (100-66.70))/2 = 50.35%, matching Acts Desc's actual 50.65% to
within 0.3pp.

### Finding 4 — the averaging convention already cancels a target-independent bias, but not perfectly

If the auditor guesses "female" with fixed probability `p`, independent of target and
content, then female-target accuracy = `p`, male-target accuracy = `1-p`, and their
average is exactly 50% regardless of `p`. This is why `io`'s published average (50.20%)
and `base_model_control`'s (50.35%) land close to 50% despite very lopsided per-gender
numbers - averaging both genders, the convention used throughout `METRICS.md`, already
functions as a bias-cancellation mechanism, whenever the bias itself doesn't depend on
which checkpoint generated the evidence.

**Consequence:** Acts Desc's 50.65% average is only 0.65pp above the pure-bias
prediction - its true informativeness, net of bias, is close to indistinguishable from
zero. Acts Tokens' 60.25% (+10.25pp above the bias-neutral point) and SAE Desc's 87.80%
(+37.80pp) carry real signal well beyond what cancelled bias alone would produce.
**Distance from 50%, not the raw average, is the honest per-method signal measure** -
and it isn't a perfect fix either: the real-but-abstract numbers in Finding 3 (60.0% vs
68.2%) show the bias is not perfectly target-independent once real (not null) content is
involved, so some residual uncancelled bias likely survives even in headline averages.

### Finding 5 — dataset balance is exact by design, not sampled

`direct` set evaluation always uses the same 100 prompts x 2 target-gender checkpoints
(female-tuned, male-tuned) x 10 sampled responses = 1000 items per checkpoint, verified
identical prompt sets across both files. A designed evaluation grid, not an
organically-collected dataset that could carry its own class imbalance - "dataset skew"
is not a separate concern from the auditor-bias findings above.

### Finding 6 — does Finding 3's calibration result generalize beyond one evidence template?

**Question.** Finding 3 measured target-independent null-content bias for one template
(`acts_desc`). Before treating "averaging cancels the bias" as a general property of the
evaluation protocol rather than a one-template coincidence, check whether it holds for
the templates the actual `METRICS.md` methods use.

**Test.** Same null-content construction as Finding 3, applied to two more templates:
the ranked-token-list template (shared verbatim by Acts Tokens, LL Tokens, and SAE
Tokens - confirmed by reading their auditor scripts, so one measurement covers all
three) and the SAE-feature-list template (SAE Desc).

| template | female-tuned: guessed "female" | male-tuned: guessed "female" | gap | z | null avg |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| token list (Acts/LL/SAE Tokens) | 90.10% | 88.60% | 1.50pp | 1.09 (n.s.) | 50.75% |
| SAE-feature list (SAE Desc) | 87.40% | 85.70% | 1.70pp | 1.11 (n.s.) | 50.85% |

**Result.** Both land within noise of target-independence, same as Finding 3's template
- confirmed on 3/3 templates checked now, not 1/3. Distance-from-bias-neutral for the
real methods using these templates:

| method | null avg | real avg | distance = real signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Acts Tokens | 50.75% | 60.25% | +9.50pp |
| SAE Desc | 50.85% | 87.80% | **+36.95pp** |
| Acts Desc (Finding 1) | 50.35% | 50.65% | +0.30pp |

**Reading.** SAE Desc's null-content bias (86.55% avg magnitude) is comparable to Acts
Tokens' (89.35%) - so SAE Desc's much larger real-world lead is not explained by a
friendlier template baseline; it reflects substantially more real informativeness
against a similarly strong default. This also means the project's own headline
cross-method ranking (SAE Desc > Acts Tokens) survives the confound Finding 1-3 raised -
the concern was legitimate to check, and checking it strengthens rather than undermines
the existing ranking. See `FINDINGS.md` for the precise scope of what this does and does
not establish (confirmed for null content on 3 templates; real-but-ambiguous content
still shows the symmetry-breaking effect from Finding 2).

## Decisions

- Kept the Acts Desc evidence/audit files as new directories under
  `experiments/acts_desc/` rather than overwriting anything, consistent with
  session 4's "never overwrite a diagnosis stage" convention.
- Added `acts_desc` as a new mode to the shared `utils/guess_secret_word.py`
  rather than writing a standalone auditor script, to keep the auditor call itself
  exactly reused per CLAUDE.md - the change is additive (one new mode branch, one new
  passthrough field) and mirrors the existing `patchscope_description` mode's shape.
- Chose to broadcast one paraphrased description per unique prompt across its 10
  response-index items, rather than paraphrasing all ~990 items individually, after
  verifying (not just assuming) that the underlying token evidence barely varies across
  a prompt's responses - this matches session 4's own established causal-masking
  argument (pronoun-position activations can't depend on the model's later response) and
  kept the paraphrase step to ~200 LLM calls instead of ~2000.
- Built the null-placeholder calibration control as a direct, cheap answer to a user
  question ("how would you even measure the bias number?") rather than treating it as
  out of scope - judged in-budget because it's a ~15min GPU job that materially changes
  how *all* of this project's prior headline numbers should be interpreted, not just this
  session's.
