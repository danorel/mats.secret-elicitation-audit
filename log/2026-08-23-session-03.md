## Findings

### Finding 1 — does the Patchscope injection mechanism itself work, and does `gender_direction` decode through it?

**Symptom that triggered the investigation.** All 6 variants
(contrastive/random_control/base_model_control × female/male) collapsed to the exact
same accuracy at layer 24. Reading the raw descriptions showed why: the model was
describing the literal "X" character from the few-shot template's own strong prior,
regardless of what vector was patched in, at up to 20x natural injection scale - a
templating artifact, not evidence about the direction.

**Test 1 — does the injection mechanism work at all?** Patch a real word embedding
(not `gender_direction`) into the same position, as a positive control.

**Result 1.** Confirmed working: " woman" → `"a human female"` at layer 5. But the
effect dilutes by layer 10 and is gone entirely by layer 15 with the definitional
template - a real property of Gemma-2's residual stream at that template position, not a
bug in the injection code.

**Test 2 — does a better template restore the mechanism at the layers that matter?**
Switched to the classic Patchscopes induction template
(`"dog -> dog\nocean -> ocean\nhappiness -> happiness\nX ->"`).

**Result 2.** Clean positive controls at layer 24 (" woman"→"woman", " king"→"king") -
the mechanism itself works fine at this layer with the right template. But
`gender_direction` itself still decodes to template artifacts or "unknown" at every
injection scale tried, on the same template where real words decode correctly.

**Conclusion: a clean negative, not a bug.** The mechanism is verified correct with two
templates and two independent positive controls, isolating the failure to the direction
vector itself. `gender_direction` carries real classification signal at the
projection-sign level (beats both controls at 70.45% via nearest-token decode) but isn't
a vector that decodes into vocabulary-adjacent content via any method tried.
**Mechanism (plausible, not directly confirmed):** a diff-of-means direction averaged
over many topically-varied contrastive sentences may carry real linearly-separable
signal without the same kind of clean, single-concept vocabulary alignment that a real
token embedding or SAE feature has - it can separate the two classes without being a
point the model treats as a nameable thing.

### Finding 2 — does the contrastive-pair activation geometry show gender clustering directly?

**Test.** UMAP/PCA on the raw contrastive-pair activations (no diff-of-means
subtraction).

**Result.** No gender separation - points cluster by sentence topic instead. Expected,
and mechanistically consistent with Finding 1: between-pair (topic) variance dominates
the small within-pair gender difference that the paired diff-of-means is specifically
designed to isolate by subtraction. Confirms the method's own design choice (paired
subtraction, not a global projection) is doing necessary work, not redundant work.

### Finding 3 — does the layer-10 98.95% headline number hold up under a bug check?

**Trigger.** The result "looked too good" (CLAUDE.md's own rule: assume bug or leakage
until checked, rather than write it up on trust).

**Test.** Count how many distinct top-5 token combinations appear across all 1000
decoded items at layer 10.

**Result.** Only **2 unique top-5 token combinations across all 1000 items** (same at
layer 23). **Root cause:** the real per-item activation is used only to compute the
*sign* of `mean_proj`; the decode step then ranks nearest tokens against the *static
direction vector itself* (flipped by that sign), not against the per-item activation.
This differs fundamentally from how the paper's own LL/Acts/SAE Tokens decode - against
the real per-item activation, genuinely varying item to item. **Consequence:** the
98.95% headline number compares a near-trivial 2-way lookup against methods that require
genuine per-item inference - not apples-to-apples, and overstated. The underlying raw
*projection* separability (990/990, zero overlap between classes, both controls at
chance) is unaffected by this bug and remains real; only the auditor-accuracy framing on
top of it is inflated. Carried into session 4 as the top-priority fix.

## Issues hit

### Patchscope apply script was unbatched — ~3h for what should take ~15-20 min

- **Symptom:** the main experiment (6 apply files × 1000 items) took ~3h wall clock.
- **Root cause:** decode step called `model.generate()` once per item, no batching.
- **Fix:** split into a cheap per-item forward pass (compute signed direction vector)
  and a batched decode phase (default batch size 25). Verified byte-identical output
  before/after on a 20-item pilot — only speed changed (~10-20x on the decode phase).
  Also added `--limit N` pilot support project-wide going forward.
