## Findings

### The Patchscope injection mechanism works; `gender_direction` still doesn't decode

All 6 variants (contrastive/random_control/base_model_control × female/male) collapsed
to the exact same accuracy at layer 24 — reading raw descriptions showed why: the model
was describing the literal "X" character from the few-shot template's own strong prior,
regardless of what vector was patched in, at up to 20x natural injection scale.
Positive control (patching a real word embedding, not `gender_direction`) confirms the
mechanism itself is correct: " woman" → `"a human female"` at layer 5, but the effect
dilutes by layer 10 and is gone entirely by layer 15 with the definitional template —
a real property of Gemma-2's residual stream at that template position, not a bug.
Switching to the classic Patchscopes induction template
(`"dog -> dog\nocean -> ocean\nhappiness -> happiness\nX ->"`) restores clean positive
controls at layer 24 (" woman"→"woman", " king"→"king") — but `gender_direction` itself
still decodes to template artifacts or "unknown" at every scale tried.

**Conclusion: a clean negative, not a bug.** The mechanism is verified correct with two
templates and two independent positive controls. `gender_direction` carries real
classification signal at the projection-sign level (beats both controls at 70.45% via
nearest-token decode) but isn't a vector that decodes into vocabulary-adjacent content
via any method tried. Plausible explanation: a diff-of-means direction over many
topically-varied contrastive sentences may carry real signal without the same kind of
clean, single-concept vocabulary alignment a real token embedding or SAE feature has.

### UMAP/PCA on raw contrastive-pair activations shows no gender separation — expected

Points cluster by sentence topic, not gender — between-pair (topic) variance dominates
the small within-pair gender difference the paired diff-of-means is designed to
isolate. Confirms why the method needs paired subtraction rather than a global
projection.

### Post-session review: `top_k_tokens` collapses to a sign-only lookup

Prompted by the layer-10 result "looking too good" (CLAUDE.md: assume bug/leakage
until checked). Only **2 unique top-5 token combinations across all 1000 items** at
layer 10 (same at layer 23) — the real per-item activation is used only to compute the
sign of `mean_proj`; the decode step then ranks nearest tokens to the *static
direction vector itself* (flipped by that sign), not the per-item activation. This
differs fundamentally from how the paper's own LL/Acts/SAE Tokens decode (against the
real per-item activation, genuinely varying item to item). **The 98.95% headline
number is comparing a near-trivial 2-way lookup against methods that require genuine
per-item inference — not apples-to-apples.** The underlying raw-projection
separability (990/990, zero overlap, both controls at chance) is still real; the
auditor-accuracy framing specifically overstates it. Carried into session 4 as the top
priority fix.

## Issues hit

### Patchscope apply script was unbatched — ~3h for what should take ~15-20 min

- **Symptom:** the main experiment (6 apply files × 1000 items) took ~3h wall clock.
- **Root cause:** decode step called `model.generate()` once per item, no batching.
- **Fix:** split into a cheap per-item forward pass (compute signed direction vector)
  and a batched decode phase (default batch size 25). Verified byte-identical output
  before/after on a 20-item pilot — only speed changed (~10-20x on the decode phase).
  Also added `--limit N` pilot support project-wide going forward.
